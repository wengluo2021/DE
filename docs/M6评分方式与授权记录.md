## How sources are distinguished

- For records the user explicitly confirmed item by item, the reviewer keeps `C (the user in this chat)`, corresponding to the actual reply and explicit request, without rewriting existing decisions.
- For routine delegation records, the reviewer writes `Codex AI (C-authorized assisted grading)`, with a note explicitly stating that item-by-item human confirmation was not performed, and retains the original AI recommendation, actual citations and reasons.
- If a special item is decided by an actual person, the independent actual reply and scoring receipt are saved, and then the other routine items are handled. AI identity will not be used to impersonate a human name.

Whole answers are judged correct/partial/wrong against the necessary facts frozen under A; citations are judged by the blocks actually bound to each claim; facts explicitly given in the question may be used as context, but unreferenced legal rules may not be used to supplement the evidence. A valid chunk does not equal semantic support, and overall correctness does not mean every citation qualifies. Accepted examples and new specific reasons are both retained.

## How results are stated

Database scoring fields, the number completed, and item-by-item human verification are different concepts. The backend `review_complete` and `formal_results` indicate that its scoring fields and formal run conditions are satisfied, and cannot by themselves prove that all fields were reviewed item by item by a human.

Reports and deliverables will list the number, scope and limitations of item-by-item human confirmation and authorized AI, and will be called AI-assisted experimental scoring with mixed sources; they will not be called entirely pure human scoring, nor will AI results be treated as an independent human gold standard. The human scoring requirement in the team's original specification and the method actually used this time will be disclosed together for the team lead's review.

This authorization does not change the frozen regulations, question set, original model outputs or retrieval metrics; it does not start new 120 experiments or repeat paid tests. The rules of phased work, self-checks, handover, local branch saving, key isolation, and no push/no writing to main/no team messages continue to apply.
