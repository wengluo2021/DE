## Understanding in Chinese

The question asks: when a consumer uses a certain means of payment, may the trader charge a fee higher than the cost the trader itself incurs in accepting that means of payment? Rule A is that it may not, and Member States should prohibit such above-cost charges. It does not say that all fees must be zero.

The first claim in all four groups fully expresses Rule A, so the overall answers are all suggested as correct. The second claim restates the same prohibition rule using the recital and does not change the completeness assessment.

Among the 8 claims, the actual citation for 7 contains the complete corresponding normative sentence. Only the citation for F400 claim 1 begins with "be prohibited", missing the subject "traders should" and the modal prefix. It retains the main wording prohibiting above-cost charges, but whether the full norm is sufficiently clear still requires a specific decision by a human.

The question gives the trader/consumer subject, but the question itself asks whether something is lawful and does not directly give the normative answer that it should be prohibited. Therefore whether this truncated citation is supported should be judged separately; it cannot be completed directly by borrowing another citation from claim 0, nor can it be judged supported merely because the overall answer is correct.

## Original question and A reference

> Under Directive 2011/83/EU, may a trader charge the consumer a fee for using a particular means of payment that is higher than the trader's own cost of accepting that means?

> No. Member States must prohibit traders from charging consumers, for the use of a given means of payment, fees that exceed the cost borne by the trader for the use of that means.
