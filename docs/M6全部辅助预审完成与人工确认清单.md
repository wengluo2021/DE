## First unify the grading standard

The pre-review checks against the completeness of the A reference answer/necessary facts. Some references contain incidental qualifications that the question does not separately ask about, so some records with correct core answers are provisionally suggested as partial. For example, the application to digital-content goods in question 8, the reasonable time/significant inconvenience in question 9, and the uniform list/amendment method in question 20. The actual reviewer should first confirm whether these are all required elements and apply this uniformly across the four groups; no group may be relaxed ad hoc.

Conditional wording must also be treated uniformly: mistaking a necessary condition for a sufficient condition, an unclear reference in "This rule", a missing negation, or citing the wrong provision must not be ignored merely because other sentences are correct. In question 6, F800 applies the withdrawal refund rule to termination for late delivery; the reversed direction of "cannot" in question 20, F400, is worth reading first.

Answer completeness and citation support are judged separately. Hit-interval metrics are not answer scores; a claim being correct in the full text does not mean that the truncated citation actually bound to it is sufficient. Adjacent blocks may jointly support a claim, but they must be blocks actually cited by that claim.
